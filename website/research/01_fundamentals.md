## 1. Landing Page Structure

### 1.1 Above-the-fold & attention distribution

- **The fold still matters** — NN/g eyetracking (2018) shows attention concentrates at the top of the page. 57% of all page-viewing time is spent above the fold; 74% is spent in the first two screenfuls; 81% in the first three. (Down from 80% above-fold in their 2010 study — long pages have diluted but not killed the effect.) [NN/g: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/]
- **Top half of the viewport > bottom half**: more than 65% of above-the-fold viewing time goes to the top half of the screen (75%+ on SERPs). The closer info is to the top, the more likely it is to be read. [NN/g, same article]
- **The "fold barrier" has pushed down to ~the 3rd screenful** — 81% of viewing time is in the first three screenfuls; people rarely go beyond the third screenful; interaction cost of scrolling reduces the chance lower content is seen. [NN/g, same article]
- **Implications (NN/g guidance):**
  - Reserve the top of the page for high-priority content: key business AND user goals. Keep major CTAs above the fold.
  - Beware **false floors** — minimalist layouts that look like the page has ended (illusion of completeness blocks scrolling). Add signifiers (e.g., cut-off text, partial next section) that invite scrolling.
  - Pages today trend *too sparse*; info density is a levers either way. Test page length with real users. [NN/g, same article]
- **Unbounce ("Keep the action above the fold")**: headline + USP + a strong CTA must be visible without scrolling — but don't cram: "too much above the fold can make it difficult to see your CTA." [Unbounce: https://unbounce.com/conversion-rate-optimization/landing-page-best-practices/]
- **GoodUI nuance**: "the idea of squeezing everything 'above the fold' is fading" — long pages are normal, and it's fine to have a soft CTA at top and a prominent one at the bottom (see §3.3 repetition). [GoodUI: https://goodui.org/]

### 1.2 Hero section

- **Hero = value prop + CTA, immediately — especially on mobile.** "Above the fold is prime real estate, so I make sure the value prop and CTA show up immediately, especially on mobile." [HubSpot: https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/landing-page-best-practices]
- **Show the product in action in the hero** ("Show your product or service in action"): rather than abstract stock imagery, demonstrate the product/service doing its job; humans + real usage read as more credible. [Unbounce LP best practices]
- **Counterpoint on premature hero CTAs**: Copyhackers notes a prematurely placed hero button can undercut persuasion — "Consider removing the CTA from the hero and writing the page with a traditional copywriting formula in mind instead" for high-consideration offers. People WILL click early CTAs, but an early button can interrupt the persuasive arc. [Copyhackers: https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/]
- **Live/ambient data in the hero can substitute for reading**: a landing page with a live-updating price grid ("background became the pitch") drove scroll depth +61% in a week — turning the value prop into a visual, verifiable asset. [HubSpot expert case study]

### 1.3 Content hierarchy & section ordering

- **The canonical build order in order of conversion importance** (HubSpot): 1) Headline that clearly states value → 2) image/graphic that reinforces it → 3) quick description or bullets → 4) CTA or lead form (ideally above the fold) → 5) social proof (logos/testimonials). Everything else is optional on top of this foundation. [HubSpot]
- **The 5 things a page must cover**: pain point, your solution, features, benefits, social proof ("it's not about cramming every detail above the fold, it's about building the narrative in a way that earns trust fast and answers the biggest questions upfront"). [HubSpot]
- **Match layout to consideration level**: cheap offers → headline + a few scannable bullets + form above the fold. Complex offers (trial, consult) → layer testimonial, feature list, explainer video. "More friction? More clarity." [HubSpot]
- **The 5-second test**: "if someone skims this in 5 seconds, will they understand the value?" [HubSpot]
- **One-column narrative layout** guides readers predictably top → bottom and converges on a single CTA; multi-column risks scattering attention away from the core purpose. [GoodUI #1]
- **Money-down message-matching skip for structure**: see §2.5 (message match) — the page must continue the promise the ad made.

## 2. Copywriting

### 2.1 Value proposition

- The value proposition is "your North Star, attracting and anchoring potential customers" — not set in stone; the single most important copy block on the page. [Copyhackers: https://copyhackers.com/2023/09/copywriting-for-startups/]
- **Reusable value-prop formulas (same product, multiple phrasings)** — [Copyhackers formulas article]:
  - `For {target} who {need or opportunity}, {Name} is {product category} that {benefit}.` → "For content teams with aggressive publishing schedules and high standards, Airstory is the content creation software that helps teams build on each other's ideas to produce incredible articles, blog posts and ebooks."
  - **VAD** — `Verb, Application, Differentiator`: "Drop research directly into blog posts and articles."
  - `We do X, but the difference is {primary differentiator}.`
  - `The {adjective} way for {who} to {verb}, {outcome}.` → "The no-brainer way for writers to turn ideas into blog posts, articles and ebooks — for better content marketing."
  - `{Proven industry example} for/of {new domain}.` → "Evernote meets Google Docs, for writers."
  - `We help X do Y doing Z.` / `We're the ones that {differentiator}.`
  - `{Superlative} {category} {qualifiers}.` → "The smartest writing software for content teams."
  - **Customer-Problem-Solution**: state the problem the customer has → the solution → the mechanism.
  - **What.How.Why.** — name the product/category, what it does, then "so you can {outcome}."
  - **Enormous problem → Solution → Core differentiator → Credibility.**
- **Clarity over cleverness everywhere**: "leading with clarity, not cleverness"; fill-in-the-blank discipline: "You're here because {problem}. Here's how we fix it." [HubSpot]
- **Specificity beats generic**: "If you can articulate the problem better than your reader can, they'll trust that you can solve it too." [HubSpot]

### 2.2 Core copy frameworks

- **AID(A)** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. The D is the one most pages skip: "a lot of businesses address the A, I and A in AIDA… but forget entirely about the D." [Copyhackers formulas]
- **AIDCA** — adds **Conviction** (for skeptical audiences); **AIDPPC** (Robert Collier): Attention, Interest, Description, Persuasion, Proof, Close — use Proof where AIDA leaves it implied. [Copyhackers formulas]
- **PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution.** Dan Kennedy called it "the most reliable copywriting formula for sales ever invented"; works from tweets to long sales pages. Variation: **CSI** = Challenge → Solution → Invitation (the CTA). [Copyhackers formulas]
- **PAPA** (Problem, Advantages, Proof, Action) — well-suited to home/lead-gen pages where "painting a picture" (the 4Ps "Picture") can read as scammy. [Copyhackers formulas]
- **AICPBSSAWN** (long-form): Attention → Interest → Credibility → Proof → Benefits → **Scarcity** → Action → **Warn** (what happens if they don't act) → **Now** (motivate immediate action). [Copyhackers formulas]
- **Star Story Solution** — character-focused (early Apple ads). **Bob Stone's 7-step, Frank Egner's 9-point** for sales pages. **Perry Belcher 21-part** sales letter formula — note: CTA appears at step **#19**, after offer, bonuses, value stacking, price, scarcity and guarantee. "You should not be in a rush to supply a CTA." [Copyhackers formulas]
- **FAB on deeper sections**: Feature → Advantage → Benefit ("the prospect may be in a higher stage of awareness… she's got a checklist of must-have features"). [Copyhackers formulas]

### 2.3 Headlines — reusable formulas

- "I never write a headline without consulting a swipe file." Every subhead/crosshead deserves the same attention as the main H1. All formulas below are swipe-file-derived. [Copyhackers formulas]

**Category/template headline formulas:**
- `{Product Name} is a {product category} that {different thing it does best}` (TechCrunch-style): "Airstory is writing software that lets you send great ideas straight to your document."
- `Who Else Wants {Most Desirable Outcome or Benefit}?`
- `The Only {Keyword Phrase} Made Exclusively to {Most Desirable Outcome}`
- `Get the {Unusual Adjective} Power of {Product Category} Without {Pain}` → "Get the astonishing power of eye tracking technology… without the high costs" (Crazy Egg).
- `Get Rid of {Problem} Once and For All`
- `How to Permanently Stop {Painful/Embarrassing Thing}, Even if You've Tried Everything!`
- `{Objection}. But/And it works.` → "Ugly. And it works."
- `How to {get incredible result} and {do unexpected thing as a result}.`
- `To the {role} who will settle for nothing less than {world-class outcome}`
- `How Your {Service Provider} Is Ripping You Off. (And what to do about it right now.)`
- `I Found a {Adjective} Way to {Get Incredible Outcome}`
- `The Ultimate Guide to {Good, Bad, or Desirable Thing}` / `What Everybody Ought to Know About {Thing}`
- `X Lessons I Learned from {Person or Unusual Experience}`
- `Is {Trending Topic} a Scam?`
- `Little Known Ways to {Outcome}` / `The Secret of {Desirable Thing}`
- Curiosity/tease formats: `I Stopped {Doing Common Thing} Today. You Should Too. Here's Why.` and `I spent {time} {working toward goal}. I just {quit}. Here's why.`
- `But Here's the Twist` style: `{Surprising fact}. But here's what I'd do differently.`
- Q&A/direct-address format (still effective for services): "Tired of reconciling payroll errors every month and still feeling like something's missing?" [HubSpot example]

**Headline "contextual"/subhead treatment** — every headline-role line gets the same formula discipline. [Copyhackers formulas]

### 2.4 Bullets, specificity & benefits

- Direct-response writers call bullets **"fascinations"** — written to be so compelling they magnify desire; a plain "benefit" bullet promotes the feature, a fascination hooks emotion. [Copyhackers formulas]
- **Benefit linking**: pair every feature with its outcome — `{Feature} so you can {benefit}`. Example: "Automate your payroll audits *so you can* stop stressing over errors". [HubSpot]
- **Specificity/number specificity (GENERAL)**: concrete numbers, timeframes, and outcomes ("in 48 hours," "17 templates," "9 in 10") beat vague superlatives — specificity is a verifiability cue. [GENERAL, echoed by Copyhackers/GoodUI "precise non-rounded numbers are more believable"]
- **Assume scanning**: most users will skim, not read. NN/g: put the most important points in the first two paragraphs; use headings/subheadings; start headings with the information-bearing words (if only the first 2 words are read, readers still get the gist); bold key phrases; write descriptive link text, not "click here"/"more." [NN/g: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/]

### 2.5 Message match & clarity rules

- **Match headline/copy/design to the ad you're running** (search/social) — strongest #1 landing page best practice. Long ad → long-matching page; for many distinct ads, use variant pages or dynamic text replacement. [Unbounce]
- **Clear, compelling copy rules**: compelling headline + fewer paragraphs + more bulleted lists; test headline comprehension with people unfamiliar with the business. [Unbounce]
- **Concise copy**: short sentences, simple/fewer words, cut filler — "after the first draft, see if you can condense it." [GoodUI #69]
- **Cover the main points** (pain, solution, features, benefits, proof) and keep copy error-free and easy to scan. [HubSpot]
- Copy should "meet people where they are" — reflect the visitor's current struggle/active problem, not just product attributes. [HubSpot]

## 3. Call-to-Action (CTA) Best Practices

### 3.1 Button copy

- **Specific beats vague**: "specific CTAs like 'Get the guide' or 'Reserve your spot' almost always outperform vague ones like 'Learn more.' The clearer the next step, the more likely people are to take it." [HubSpot]
- **The "I Want" Button** (fill-in-the-blank): copy as the visitor's own words — "I want to ___" ("I want to get a new car") or "I want you to ___" ("I want you to show me the newest cars"). First-person "me" stays in the button. Start commercial buttons with "get," then match the phrase to the prospect's desire/expectation. [Copyhackers formulas]
- **RAD**: Require (visitor has info required before clicking → don't place a premature button), Acquire (make the target easy to click), Desire (the button must promise what the visitor actually wants). [Copyhackers formulas]
- **Hook, Line and Sinker** (WordStream): 1) hook with a no-BS direct CTA ("Download our free whitepaper"), 2) a low-commitment offer, 3) a closer ("click here for more resources"). [Copyhackers formulas]
- **First-person vs second-person (GENERAL, widely-tested)**: "Start my free trial" vs "Start your free trial" — the famous Michael Aagaard A/B test reported "Start my free trial" winning by a wide margin (as much as ~90% in some reported variants) across Unbounce/Certainly landing pages. Labeled GENERAL because not re-fetched here; keep as a prime button-copy test candidate.
- **Don't treat buttons as microcopy**: "buttons are the site of conversion online — you can't convert without clicking at least one button." [Copyhackers formulas]

### 3.2 Contrast, size, affordance

- **CTAs should look like buttons** — bold, contrasting color, recognizable shape — so they pop out from the page. [Unbounce]
- **Raise contrast so the CTA dominates**: use tone (darker vs lighter), depth (drop shadows/gradients), or complementary color-wheel pairings. [GoodUI #11]
- **Distinct, consistent interactive styles**: clickable / selected / plain-text must be visually distinct and applied consistently (e.g., blue = clickable). Don't blur the styles. [GoodUI #6]
- **Attention grabs**: any element can be emphasized via size, contrast, irregular shapes, auto-focus, sticky/floating position, directional arrows — but "you cannot have everything scream on a page"; reserve emphasis for the primary CTA. [GoodUI #56]
- **Fitts's Law (GENERAL)**: larger click targets and closer-to-intent placement are easier/faster to hit — applies to mobile thumb zones especially.

### 3.3 Placement — folds, repetition, single vs multiple

- **One focused CTA per page beats competing asks**: "One strong CTA always outperforms multiple competing asks. If I want someone to download an ebook, I'm not also asking them to sign up for a newsletter or book a call." [HubSpot + also Unbounce remove-nav/distraction principle]
- **Repetition across the page (not per-screen)**: repeat the CTA on long pages — one soft actionable item at the top, another prominent one at the bottom ("when people reach the bottom, they pause and think what to do next — a potential solid place to make an offer or close a deal"). Don't repeat 10x on one screen. [GoodUI #5]
- **Sticky/floating CTA**: recurring mobile tests (GoodUI leaked experiments on KensingtonTours, Obs.no, Obsbygg.no) treat the **sticky bottom CTA** as a primary pattern for mobile lead-gen; currently being validated via the Trustworthy Replication Project. [GoodUI leaked tests]
- **Above/below the fold**: keep the major CTA above the fold [NN/g, Unbounce], but for high-consideration offers, delay the hero CTA until the page has made its case (Copyhackers' #19-of-21 position; RAD formula). Balance per offer type. [Copyhackers formulas]
- **Minimize competing links above the CTA**: on tunnel-style pages, every link above the primary CTA risks leaking the visitor away from the goal; more links = more exits. [GoodUI #16]
- **Action-oriented vs passive language**: be direct and confident — avoid "perhaps," "maybe," "interested?", "want to?"; tell people what to do next. [GoodUI #10] Use action verbs tied to the outcome ("Get," "Start," "Reserve," "Download"). [HubSpot]

### 3.4 CTA microcopy / "click triggers"

- **Click triggers** = the small lines around the CTA that ease hesitation at the decision moment; a single line under the button ("Only takes 30 seconds") can meaningfully lift conversion. Working examples: "Free download, no signup required," "Join 10,000+ marketers using this template," "Limited spots available," "Cancel anytime, no strings attached," money-back guarantee, easy unsubscribe, happy-customer quote, "what to expect" blurb, price slashing, privacy policy. "The trigger must match the offer and never overpromise." [HubSpot]

## 4. Conversion Psychology

### 4.1 Social proof — types, strength, placement

- **Mechanism**: social proof "creates or reinforces trust and drives users to action via the persuasive power of herd behavior… and psychological phenomena that drive normative behavior." Types: testimonials (written/video), user reviews, customer ratings, embedded social mentions ("Customers also bought," counts/counters), "as seen in" logos. [Copyhackers: https://copyhackers.com/2023/04/social-proof/]
- **Evidence (Copyhackers' 5 studies):**
  1. **Hospital hand-washing field study** (14 weeks, 7 signs, n=246,098): each sign mapped to one of Cialdini's seven principles. Only the **Authority** and **Social Proof** signs significantly increased hand washing vs no sign. Notably, the winning social-proof message wasn't a testimonial or review — "Our hospital visitors disinfect their hands" — pure herd/bandwagon. [Copyhackers social proof]
  2. **eID adoption**: adding a social-proof callout made people **3.96x more likely** to adopt digital ID; in the tested pair of screens, 77% opted for the eID option where the callout was present. [Copyhackers social proof]
  3. **Social proof works hardest when people are conflicted/unsure**: in a grocery-choice study for meat products (ambivalent consumers), higher ambivalence → social proof (e.g., ">82% rejection" bars) correlated with higher rejection behavior; and **decisions were made faster when social proof was present** (reduces uncertainty). Corollary: a nudge is pointless when the person already knows what they want, and ineffective when they think they're smarter than the herd. [Copyhackers social proof]
  4. **What shoppers rank first on B2C sites**: (1) fast & accurate search, (2) **reviews by verified buyers**, (3) digital security certificates, (4) privacy policies, (5) trust factors like online support. Essential usability was the ONLY factor ranked above verified-buyer reviews — i.e., next to basic usability, verified reviews are the strongest trust/conversion lever. [Copyhackers social proof]
  5. **"AI proof"**: 85% of participants agreed with a humanoid robot's assessment — social proof-style shortcuts work even when the "herd" is an authority-like AI. Implies the nudge is about decision shortcuts, not just numbers. [Copyhackers social proof]
- **Make testimonials feel real (authenticity)**: include full names, job titles, place of residence, purchase date, biographical detail, portraits, or video. [Unbounce]
- **Testimonial writing formulas** [Copyhackers formulas]: TEASE (short-form testimonial structure); the **4 Ss** (AWAI): an accurate, specific testimonial following a simple S-pattern; and story-form testimonials (show the initial belief → the transformation → the result). Gather raw customer feedback first, then edit with client sign-off.
- **Placement**: logos/testimonials as the optional-but-high-value 5th element of the core page structure [HubSpot]; "customers also bought" behavior in flow. [Copyhackers social proof]
- **Contextual caution (GENERAL + Cialdini)**: social proof must be *relevant* to the audience, *verifiable*, and consistent with the offer — fake or mismatched proof backfires (see §5 authenticity).

### 4.2 Scarcity & urgency

- **Mechanism**: scarcity ("Only 5 items left!") and urgency ("Sale ends in 3 hours!") trigger FOMO; create perceived value increase (limited = valuable) and prompt decision-making by minimizing procrastination. [Copyhackers startups]
- **Use judiciously** — two hard rules: authenticity (real discounts, real stock) and avoid overuse (constant "last chance" messaging desensitizes and erodes trust). [Copyhackers startups]
- **Scarcity is one of the deliberate late-page moves** in long-form formulas (AICPBSSAWN "Scarcity"; 21-part formula injects scarcity right before price/guarantee/CTA). [Copyhackers formulas]
- **FOMO framing in VSL templates**: "warn of FOMO with the looming deadline and scarcity" as a final-CTA device. [Copyhackers formulas]

### 4.3 Authority

- **Credentials step is explicit in persuasion formulas**: "list off your credentials, including proof, results, examples, testimonials" (VSL template); "present your credentials" (Perry Belcher); authority is one of Cialdini's principles and one of the two signs that measurably worked in the hospital field study. [Copyhackers formulas + social proof]
- **Form 2 of the effective signals**: expertise claims, "as seen in" logos, author/expert bylines, certifications — [GENERAL authority signals]

### 4.4 Reciprocity

- **Give a gift before closing a sale**: gifting is a persuasion tactic built on the **rule of reciprocity** — "being nice to someone by offering a small token of appreciation can come back in your favour down the road." [GoodUI #2]
- **General forms**: lead magnets, free guides/tools/templates, valuable content before asks, free trials. [GENERAL]

### 4.5 Loss aversion

- **Core concept (Kahneman–Tversky prospect theory, GENERAL)**: losses loom ~2x larger than equivalent gains — "warn" steps ("what happens if you don't act") are standard in conversion formulas (AICPBSSAWN's Warn; 21-part "give a warning"; VSL "FOMO/deadline"). [Copyhackers formulas + GENERAL]
- Apply by framing outcomes as protection/cost-avoidance as well as gains: "stop wasting money / stop stressful errors" beats "save money / gain peace" in many audiences. [GENERAL]

### 4.6 Anchoring

- **Price anchoring (GENERAL)**: first-seen prices anchor subsequent judgments — show the higher/regular or premium price before the actual one.
- **Price illusions that shape valuation (GoodUI)**: framing words ("only," "affordable," "a small fee of"); breaking price into per-unit/per-day terms ($1/day vs $30/month; 30¢/page vs $30/book); charm pricing (ending in 9); fewer digits ($30 vs $30.00). [GoodUI #51]
- **Anchoring in value stacking**: build up value of solution + bonuses *before* revealing price (21-part formula). [Copyhackers formulas]

### 4.7 Hick's law — choice reduction

- **Less choice aids decisions**: "the more choices there are, the harder decision-making becomes" — with more choice, satisfaction drops as expectation, regret and self-blame rise. [GoodUI #75, citing ConversionXL]
- **But test it**: the famous jam study (fewer jams → more purchases) has struggled to replicate; GoodUI's position is a middle ground. [GoodUI #75]
- **Recommend a default option**: when showing multiple offers, emphasize one recommended option to battle analysis paralysis. [GoodUI #7]
- **Limit comparison load**: on comparisons/pricing tables, limit the number of properties compared, space them clearly, and ask for a single decision. [GoodUI #57]

### 4.8 Von Restorff effect (isolation)

- **The isolated item stands out (GENERAL)**: an element that differs from its surroundings (in color, shape, size) gets disproportionately more attention — the psychological foundation for making one CTA "pop" via contrast. [GENERAL + GoodUI #11/#56 mechanics]

### 4.9 Cognitive load reduction

- **Progressive disclosure**: reveal information gradually (esp. in forms) — "only show/ask for what is relevant to the situation at hand." [GoodUI #43]
- **Explain, don't assume**: contextual hints/explanations for inputs remove uncertainty — but do **not** use placeholder text as the explanation (Jakob Nielsen warning). [GoodUI #68]
- **Inline validation** beats delayed errors: catch errors as they happen, in context, instead of forcing recall after submit. [GoodUI #22]
- **Whitespace and hierarchy reduce load**: spacing, bold headers, bullets make pages scannable; clutter and mixed messages are what visitors notice. [HubSpot; Copyhackers startups lesson 4]
- **Concise copy** (short sentences, simple words). [GoodUI #69]
- **Useful calculations**: the UI should do the math ("3 min ago" > absolute timestamps; remaining-days calculations). [GoodUI #53]

### 4.10 Decoy effect

- **Decoy pricing (GENERAL)**: add a deliberately inferior (or overpriced) middle option to make the target option look better value — classic good-better-best pricing uses the "better" plan as the anchor and a decoy "premium" to justify it. Related to anchoring + choice reduction above. [GENERAL; complement GoodUI #7/#75]

### 4.11 Other proven micro-mechanisms (GoodUI)

- **Smaller commitments** ("foot in the door"): a series of small yesses beats one big ask; "no contract" messaging lowers barrier. [GoodUI #44]
- **But-You-Are-Free / reaffirming choice**: explicitly saying "it's your choice / you are free to refuse" can lift compliance (some studies ~2x); strongest face-to-face and adjacent to the request. [GoodUI #54]
- **Curiosity loops**: teasers create desire — but only if you don't give away the whole answer ("keep them hungry for at least a bit"). [GoodUI #49]
- **Variable rewards** hook users (intermittent reinforcement). [GoodUI #55]
- **Set completion / goal gradient**: progress indicators and collection/goal framing motivate ("step X of Y," finite collectible sets). [GoodUI #58, #59]
- **Authenticity beats polish-of-lies**: stock photos with fake smiles undermine trust; a mix of good and bad reviews can help; precise non-round numbers read as more believable. [GoodUI #65]
- **Storytelling** over raw fact-listing is more memorable and emotional. [GoodUI #64]

## 5. Trust & Credibility

- **Trust is built through clarity, tone, and polish** — sloppy formatting, broken links, inconsistent messaging are instant red flags. [HubSpot]
- **Privacy reassurance on forms is expected**: use a privacy policy link/microcopy ("We'll never share your email" statements; notice near the form). [Unbounce lead-gen section]
- **Security signals rank high with shoppers**: after usability and verified reviews, shoppers value **digital security certificates** and **privacy policies** above other trust factors (online support last). [Copyhackers social proof study 4]
- **Trust badges**: general category of proof (SSL/payment badges, certifications) — [GENERAL] with the priority hierarchy above. HTTPS is the baseline default for any modern site. [GENERAL]
- **Guarantees are risk-removers in every big persuasion formula**: "reduce risk with a guarantee" and "create a great guarantee… this critical risk-reducer" appear near the decision point (before the CTA) in both the 21-part and 12-step templates — because guarantees are a specific form of click trigger. [Copyhackers formulas + HubSpot click triggers]
- **Transparent pricing beats the scramble**: pricing transparency + anchoring artfully (see §4.6); lead with the value, reveal price late but clearly, and pair with risk-reversal. [Copyhackers formulas; GENERAL]
- **"As seen in" logos**: form of authority/social proof — used as the optional 5th element of the core landing structure. [HubSpot + GENERAL]
- **Authentic testimonials**: real names, job titles, locations, dates, photos/video — unverifiable or stock-looking proof backfires. [Unbounce + GoodUI #65]
- **Message mismatch kills trust**: the page must follow through on the ad's promise or the visitor bails. [Unbounce #1]

## 6. Forms

### 6.1 Field reduction

- **Fewer fields convert better**: "each field you ask for runs the risk of making your visitors turn around and give up" — question necessity of every field; move optional fields after submission. [GoodUI #13]
- **Trim to essentials for speed**: if submission rate underperforms, "start with the form… trimming it down to the essentials (like just name and email) makes a big difference." [HubSpot]
- **Trade-off**: fewer fields reduce friction, but "sometimes one extra qualifier helps lead quality" — balance volume vs. quality. [HubSpot]

### 6.2 Multi-step vs single-step

- **Multi-step forms reduce friction for lead-gen**: "nobody likes paperwork… the same goes for long forms" — splitting into steps also structures data collection so you gather info incrementally. [Unbounce lead-gen]
- **Multi-step = goal-gradient wins**: progress ("step 1 of X") leverages set-completion motivation. [GENERAL + GoodUI #58/#59]
- **Progressive disclosure** overlaps with multi-step: show only relevant fields/segments. [GoodUI #43]
- **Don't over-split [GENERAL caution]**: each extra step adds a boundary; keep steps few, labeled, and reversible. (NN/g guidance on progressive forms: keep it short and show progress.) [GENERAL]

### 6.3 Form placement & friction

- **Form or CTA ideally above the fold** (esp. mobile), as step 4 of the core structure. [HubSpot]
- **Reduce friction with microcopy / click triggers**: privacy line, "only takes 30 seconds," "no spam," "cancel anytime" near the submit. [HubSpot + Unbounce]
- **Explain fields with context** (what's needed, example input, why) — never placeholder-as-label only. [GoodUI #68]
- **Inline validation** as errors happen; enable corrections/retries without wiping input. [GoodUI #22, #72]
- **Make math easy**: no manual calculations by the user; autocomplete/format-aware fields [GENERAL + GoodUI #53].
- **Thank-you/re-engagement after submit**: a thank-you page/popup confirms submission (a step some pages forget) and opens next-action opportunities. [Unbounce lead-gen]
- **Watch drop-off beyond submission rate**: track scroll depth, time on page, and form drop-off rate, not just headline conversion rate. [Unbounce metrics]
